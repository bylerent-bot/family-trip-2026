Belize

Placencia, on the southern coast

Placencia beach. Photo: Jim McIntosh, CC0 (source)

A barefoot little fishing-village town on a thin strip of sand, with the barrier reef out front and jungle, Maya ruins and howler monkeys an hour or two inland. The idea would be a six-bedroom beach house with a pool, a pier, kayaks, golf carts and a chef included every other day, and a short hop on a small plane from the international airport so we never go into Belize City. Mom and whoever is certified could dive a reef she hasn't done yet, and the kids would have calm water inside the reef, a little island beach day and the monkeys. There's no surf, though, and the house we found is 8 miles up the road from the village.

What's nice about it

  • The diving and snorkeling might be the most of any of the options. The Silk Cayes and Glover's Reef are day boats from the house for Mom and whoever is certified, with the snorkelers on the same boat, and Laughing Bird Caye is a national park 11 miles out.
  • It's the only one with jungle and ruins too: Monkey River for howler monkeys and manatees, the Cockscomb jaguar preserve trails, and the Maya sites at Nim Li Punit and Lubaantun about two hours' drive south.
  • Mission Bay, the house we'd probably ask about first, has six bedrooms with a real bunk room, a pool by the sand, a pier, kayaks and a golf cart, and a chef comes with the rent for one full day out of every two. It's rated 9.8 out of 10 from 102 reviews.
  • Placencia Village has a pedestrian Sidewalk about three quarters of a mile long, with cafes, gelato, little shops and restaurants. From the house you could also walk to the Naia spa, the Maya Beach bistro and Inky's Mini Golf.
  • The water stays warm, about 81 degrees, and inside the reef it's flat and shallow for the kids.
  • Roughly $27,000 to $58,000 for ten, with the house as the big unknown since no holiday rate is posted yet.

The catches

  • It's the longest travel day of the options, about 10.5 to 13.5 hours each way. One connection into Belize City, then a 25 to 45 minute flight on a small plane to the Placencia airstrip. Mom might count that hop as a second stop.
  • The State Department has Belize at Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution, for crime, reissued March 12, 2026. The Level 3 area is Southside Belize City, which we wouldn't go near. Worth reading the advisory ourselves, the link is below.
  • A state of emergency ran from May 8 to about early June 2026 in Belize City and Belize District, including Ladyville, where the international airport is. It ended (the UK took its notice down June 12) and we found no renewal through October 21. Placencia was never part of it, and we'd stay inside the airport between flights.
  • On the peninsula itself: in February 2025 a US developer was shot dead outside his villa project at Cocoplum on Maya Beach, which police called a targeted hit tied to a fraud dispute. That's the same development Mission Bay is in. In March 2026 a man was stabbed to death by a neighbor in the Maya Beach area, which police said was not gang-related. In October 2023 armed men tied up the guard at the Placencia airstrip and stole a small plane, which crashed, and police suspected a drug flight. In March 2023 robbers hit a beach house at a Seine Bight resort and traded fire with police, with no one hurt. We found no 2026 shootings in Placencia.
  • Medical care is thin. The Placencia Polyclinic near the airstrip is billed as 24-hour, though local reporting found it unstaffed on one visit, and it's about 8 miles from the house. The nearest hospital is in Dangriga, about 1 to 1.5 hours away, and anything serious would mean a flight to Belize City. We'd bring epinephrine from home.
  • No six-bedroom house turned up inside the village walk. Mission Bay is 8 miles north, roughly 25 to 35 minutes by golf cart by our guess, on the one peninsula road, which is unlit at night.
  • No surf to speak of, since the reef blocks the swell.
  • December northers can bring a gray, windy day or two and cancel the outer-reef boat days. 2026 has also been a record sargassum year, and the east-facing beaches where the houses sit can get seaweed.
  • The Blue Hole is 3.5 hours each way by boat from Placencia, so that would be a long day just for the certified divers.
  • Every house is request-only or shows no calendar for December 27 to January 3, and late October is late to be asking about a holiday six-bedroom.

Weather that week. Warm and mostly sunny, with highs around 81, nights around 68 to 72 and the sea about 81. Expect passing showers most days, with sources disagreeing on how much rain (somewhere from 3 to 7 inches over the month). Now and then a norther comes through and makes it cooler, breezy and choppy for a day or two. Late December is the start of the busy season.

What about Ambergris Caye?

The reef starts right at the dock of a six-bedroom villa with a pool, Hol Chan and Shark Ray Alley are about 15 minutes away by boat, and Mom could reach the Blue Hole in about two hours instead of three and a half, so it would probably be the easier dive week. The hop from the international airport is only 15 minutes, and the island has a private hospital with a decompression chamber. Town is a 15 to 20 minute golf-cart ride, a bit closer than Placencia but still not a walk. The island has had a rougher 2026 for crime, which we'd want to read up on, and the house we found has mixed reviews. Roughly $28,500 to $49,000 for ten, with no holiday rate posted for the house yet.

Puerto Rico

Isla Verde, on the beach just east of San Juan

Isla Verde beach. Photo: P. Hughes, CC BY 4.0 (source)

A week on what seems like the calmest sand beach around San Juan, in a six-bedroom pool house about three minutes' walk from the water and roughly ten minutes from the airport, so no long drive at either end. Pine Grove, at the west end of Isla Verde, sits behind a reef well offshore that takes the edge off the winter swell, so on most days the kids could wade with a grownup close by and the adults could swim. The walk out the door is a busy hotel and restaurant avenue, with casinos and some chains mixed in, rather than a little town square, so Old San Juan, about 20 to 25 minutes away, would be our walking day. The surfers would have reef breaks 10 to 20 minutes from the house, the dive boats, the catamaran, the bio bay and El Yunque are out east toward Fajardo, and there are calm-water spots in the city for the big-swell days.

What's nice about it

  • The beach at the door might be the calmest sand in the San Juan area. Pine Grove is a sand beach and sandbar break, with the reef sitting well offshore around a little cay, and surf-forecast.com says it's too small to surf 73 percent of the time in December. That should mean a gentle shore most days.
  • Balneario de Carolina, about a kilometer east along the same beach, has lifeguards and a small kids' water park. It's listed as open Tuesday to Sunday, 8 to 5, and closed Mondays.
  • Villa Violeta and Villa Catalina are six-bedroom houses with private pools, about 3 minutes on foot from Pine Grove and 2 to 9 minutes from restaurants. Both are rated 9.6 out of 10, and Catalina has a real bunk room for the kids.
  • The JetBlue nonstop from Norfolk lands about ten minutes from the house, so there's no long transfer at either end.
  • The surfers could drive about 10 minutes to Aviones or 20 to La Ocho, Mom and whoever is certified could do a two-tank boat dive out of Fajardo, and the same coast has the Icacos catamaran, the bio bay and El Yunque. Old San Juan's forts and streets are about 20 to 25 minutes away.
  • On a big-swell day there are calm-water spots in the city, at Escambrón, Playita del Condado and the Condado Lagoon, about 15 to 20 minutes west.
  • US soil, with dollars, phones and US hospitals about 15 minutes away.
  • Roughly $28,000 to $54,000 for ten, with the house as the big unknown since no holiday rate is posted.

The catches

  • The walk out the door is a resort avenue, not a town. Isla Verde Avenue mixes some good local places with hotel casinos and chains like Denny's and Burger King, so the strolling-around-town part of the week would be Old San Juan.
  • Pine Grove itself has no lifeguards. They're at Balneario de Carolina, a short walk east, and we couldn't open the page with its hours, so we'd confirm them. On the Pine Grove sand, a grownup would want to stay within arm's reach of Taylor.
  • Nobody we found has described the seabed itself. Some guides mention scattered shells and small rocks in places, so water shoes for the kids and a look along the waterline on the first day seem like a good idea.
  • Late December is the middle of north-swell season, and a big swell still reaches the bay. On a high rip-current day (the National Weather Service posts it every morning), the swim would move to Escambrón, Playita del Condado or the lagoon, a 15 to 20 minute drive. Ocean Park, Condado and Piñones nearby aren't December beaches for little ones. The weather service lists rip-current deaths at all three, including Ocean Park on January 1, 2026, though we haven't double-checked those tables.
  • The surf at the door is small most of December, clean only about 5 percent of the time, so it's more for a lesson on a small day. The surfers would mostly drive to Aviones or La Ocho.
  • No holiday rate, minimum stay or calendar showed on any of the houses. Three of them look like they share an owner, so one inquiry might price all three, but if that owner is booked, all three are. A holiday rate over $3,000 a night would push the top of the budget toward $60,000.
  • Villa Catalina has one recent 4 out of 10 review that mentioned the air conditioning not working, a dirty pool and a slow host. None of the houses confirms a pool fence, though one Catalina photo caption mentions a white one.
  • The houses are right next to the airport, so there might be jet noise. That's our guess, since no listing or review we read mentions it.
  • The dive boats leave from Fajardo, about an hour each way by our estimate, and December swell can cancel them. We don't know yet whether non-divers can ride along. The Icacos catamaran needs 12 people to sail a shared trip, so we'd either charter it for the ten of us or join another group.
  • New Year's week is busy. Pine Grove and the balneario are among the most crowded beaches in the metro area, El Yunque's El Portal visitor center is closed on New Year's Day, and the La Mina trail is closed for construction.
  • For walks after dinner, one guide calls the Isla Verde hotel zone generally safe, thinning out toward the highway, so we'd keep to the lit avenue and the hotel fronts and stay off the beach late. Carolina closed the avenue to through traffic late on Thursday to Sunday nights after some incidents, but the article is undated, so it may not still be in place.
  • Sargassum and Portuguese man o' war are possible, and all the drive times here are our estimates, since we haven't mapped them.

Weather that week. Highs around 83, lows around 73 to 74, and about 2.8 inches of rain over roughly six days in December, usually passing showers. The sea is about 81 to 82 degrees. Late December is the heart of north-swell season, so we'd expect a big-swell day or two, and the trade winds usually pick up by late morning, so mornings would be the time to swim and surf.

What about Luquillo?

We spent a while on Luquillo, out on the northeast coast about 45 minutes from the airport. Its big public beach, Balneario La Monserrate, comes up everywhere as calm and shallow behind an offshore reef, with lifeguards. Right next to it is the kiosk strip, about sixty little open-air restaurants, which looked like a lot of fun for lunches. El Yunque would be about 10 minutes away and the Fajardo boats about 30.

The thing we kept bumping into is that the six-bedroom house on Playa Fortuna loses most of its own sand in the winter, and one guest had to climb over boulders to get to the water. So the swimming beach would be a 15 to 20 minute walk, or a quick drive once you add the kids and the beach gear. If a big house turns up closer to the balneario, we might look at it again.

What about Dorado?

We also looked at Dorado, about 40 minutes west of the airport, where the Ritz-Carlton Reserve and some beautiful villas are. The public beach there, Nolo Morales, is sand with a roped swim area and lifeguards. It faces the winter north swell straight on, though, so we could see several red-flag days in our week, and the beaches right in front of the villas aren't really meant for swimming.

The villas we found open for our dates were five bedrooms and ran about $22,000 to $44,000 for the house alone. They're in a gated resort where you'd mostly drive or take a golf cart to dinner. It feels more like a pool-and-resort week than a beach week, which could be nice on a different trip.

Weather that week. Real winter. December highs are about 27 degrees with lows about 14, and January highs are about 19 with lows about 5. Snow is likely. Wind can close the summit lifts and leave the lower half open.
